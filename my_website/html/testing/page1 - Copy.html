History & Origin

An Overview of the inception and release of the Mk1 MX-5

The NA was first conceived in the early 1980s by automotive journalist Bob Hall alongside Mazda's Kenichi Yamamoto, they wanted to emulate a spiritual successor to post-war British roadsters such as the MGB and the Lotus Elan, which inspired much of the styling.

Following this, there was a design competition between Mazda's studios in Japan and California, with the design from the American side featuring the now iconic FR layout and curved design winning the selection.

The primary design of the car was envisioned by Mazda designers Tom Matano, Mark Jordan, & Shunji Tanaka, who made sure to stick to Mazda's key design principle of 'Jinba Ittai', the Japanese concept meaning "horse and rider as one", which focuses on pure driver engagement and being able to feel the road beneath you, rather than pure speed and power.

The car was then unveiled at the world-famous Chicago Auto Show (pictured to the right) in early 1989 to rave reviews from the press attending. The car itself was known by three different marks around the world, in Japan, Mazda released it under the Eunos badge, in an attempt to launch a premium line separate to their main badge, similar to what Toyota do with their Lexus brand. However in the rest of the world it was released under the Mazda badge, in the UK and Europe as the Mazda MX-5, and in North America as the Mazda Miata, derived from the Old High German word for "reward" or "prize", an idea brought by the car's developer Bob Hall.
